A dreamlike short film following Jagan through a reality where his greatest loss never happened. Written, directed and edited by Rudeep Ramesh.

Overview

In a fragile, dreamlike reality, Jagan drifts through a world where his greatest loss never happened, but the truth pressing at the edges threatens to shatter the refuge he's built. Can he find his way back, or lose himself in it forever? Aantharam was a chance to explore narrative filmmaking end to end, from script to final cut, shaping a single vision across writing, direction and edit.

The Challenge

Aantharam came with real constraints. The entire film had to be captured in a single day: one shoot permission, no room for reshoots. The budget was tight, the crew deliberately small, and the kit pared down to only the essentials. The cast were first-time screen actors, and with no built sets, every location had to work exactly as found. The challenge was to turn all of that into an advantage: to plan tightly enough that limitation became discipline rather than compromise.

Approach

With no margin for error on a single-day shoot, everything rode on preparation. The film was fully planned in pre-production (shot list, blocking and schedule locked before arriving on location), so the one day could be spent shooting, not deciding. Locations were chosen to work exactly as found, turning real spaces into the film's world instead of built sets. With first-time actors, I kept the direction simple and the environment relaxed, rehearsing key beats so performances felt natural on camera. The small crew and minimal kit kept the set nimble and fast to move: fewer moving parts, quicker setups, more takes where they mattered.

Outcome

Aantharam was completed on schedule, proving that a clear vision and disciplined planning matter more than budget or gear. It became my first fully self-directed narrative short.
